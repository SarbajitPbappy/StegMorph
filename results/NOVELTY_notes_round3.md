## MOLSBM FULL READ (12 Nov 2026, from Paper/MOLSBM_*.pdf)
Method: per block, NSGA-II picks an 8-bit seed for rand()/randperm (embedding order) and a +/-1 pattern for mismatched pixels. Objective 1 = number of LSB mismatches (i.e. fewer modifications), objective 2 = cover-vs-stego block-histogram L1 difference. Seed is sent to the receiver (8 bits per block).
Evaluation: NRCS natural scenes, 500 images for security, payload 0.3/0.5/0.8 bpp, baselines LSBM, MKGM [7], 2GA [17] (all GA-LSBM variants, MKGM/GLSBM by the same first author). Detectors: ker1, ker2, CNGL, ALE only, all LSBM-specific hand-crafted attacks. No SRM/SPAM/CNN, no medical images, no random-seed control, no matched-operator control, one run, no CIs. Table IV values are reported as "AUC" but range 0.005 to 0.50, below chance, so the metric is not clearly AUC (unclear; do not quote as AUC).
Implications for us:
1. It is prior art for "GA-chosen seed/order per block claims better security than LSBM under steganalysis". So our claim must NOT be worded as the first test of GA key selection against steganalysis. It does not pre-empt the controlled negative result (random-key + operator-matched control, SRM/CNN, MRI, subject-disjoint).
2. Its objective 1 minimises the number of modifications, so any security gain it shows is confounded with operator/change-count (fewer changes), which is exactly the confound our RANDMIX control isolates. Good motivating citation, framed carefully: "gain is not separated from change count" (their experiments do not include such a control).
3. New closest prior art to chase: Sabeti GLSBM/MKGM [7] (sid.ir/paper/228423, GA + multi-key LSBM) and 2GA [17] (J. Soft Comput. Inf. Technol. 2020). Check whether either has a random-key baseline. Not read.
Verdict update: narrow claim stays OPEN-PROVISIONAL; MOLSBM no longer a blocker for the narrow wording; the broad wording is ruled out. literature_matrix.csv updated. Damghani 2021 PDF not supplied (still abstract-only). Still not independently verified.

Paper/Secure Image Steganography...pdf is 2GA (Sabeti & Faiazi, JSCIT 9(4):70-82); English abstract/attack list read, body Persian.
- NEW FACT (ISeCure 2026, Table 8): MOLSBM is also tested with SPAM (686), SRM (34,671), maxSRM, maxSRMd2 + ensemble classifier on 1000 NRCS images: AUC 0.54-0.57 at 0.05 bpp rising to 0.74-0.77 at 0.3 bpp. Table 8 has NO baseline row (no LSBM, no random-path, no matched control), so the table cannot show a GA benefit. Table 7 does compare against LSBM (random path/random +-1) but only with LSBM-specific attacks (ker1, ker2, CNGL, ALE).
- Thus prior art exists for: GA seed/pattern vs random-path LSBM under LSBM-specific attacks (natural images, MOLSBM, MKGM, 2GA), plus an SRM-family evaluation of the GA scheme without baseline. It does NOT do: GA vs random key (with operator-matched control) under SRM/CNN with a baseline, MRI, subject-disjoint splits, seeds/CIs, or dose-response position controls.
- Wording consequences: (a) do not claim "no one has tested GA position selection under feature detectors"; say "no baseline-controlled SRM/CNN test with operator-matched random controls was found". (b) Their LSBM baseline differs from ours (LSBM random +-1 vs our hybrid LSB/PVD with RANDSEQ/RANDMIX), so their gains under ker1/ker2 are not directly comparable. (c) Unexplained: Table 7/Table IV 'AUC' values below 0.5; do not quote.
