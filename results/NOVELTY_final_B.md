# Novelty check, final, Group B (medical and metaheuristic papers), independent second reading

Method: every page of all 11 PDFs extracted with pypdf (python -I) and read (Python 3.14 venv). No page lacked text. Pages quoted are PDF page numbers. Quotes are verbatim, 25 words or fewer. "not found in text" means absent from the extracted text. I did not open round 5 or round 6 notes until my own notes were done.

Coverage notes. (a) Özcan SVM paper: English title and abstract, Turkish body. I read the Turkish body in full (method, data, tables, results). I could read prose and tables; the DCT feature equations 1 to 7 came out as garbled glyphs, so I did not verify them. Quotes from that body are given as my English paraphrase with the Turkish key terms, not verbatim. (b) Apau SLR: all 47 pages extracted; pp.1-39 read in full including Tables 7 to 12; pp.40-47 are the reference list (checked, not content). (c) Mansour Tables I to V: numeric cells are letter-spaced digits in the extraction and the column headers are lost, so those tables are only partly readable. (d) Figures (ROC curves, images) are not recoverable.

Claim under test (narrow): GA-chosen pixel key in hybrid LSB/PVD versus RANDOM key and OPERATOR-MATCHED controls (same PVD/LSB pair counts, same modification count), under trained steganalysers (SRM/SPAM, boosted trees, full-budget CNN), brain MRI, subject-disjoint splits, 3 or more seeds, subject-cluster bootstrap CIs, plus texture-versus-smooth dose-response showing detector dependence.

## 1. Per-paper notes

### B1. Vazquez et al. 2022, file "Confidentiality in medical images through a genetic-based steganography algorithm in artificial intelligence.pdf" (10 pp.)
- Title (p.1): "Confidentiality in medical images through a genetic-based steganography algorithm in artificial intelligence". Authors: Eduardo Vazquez, Stephanie Torres, Giovanny Sanchez, Juan-Gerardo Avalos, Marco Abarca, Thania Frias, Emmanuel Juarez, Carlos Trejo, Derlis Hernandez. Venue: Frontiers in Robotics and AI 9:1031299; published 6 Oct 2022. DOI as printed (p.1): 10.3389/frobt.2022.1031299.
- Embedding: none in the usual sense. The cover is unchanged: "our method does not need to alter the original image to hide the image I in P" (p.5). A GA finds host pixels whose existing bit already equals the secret bit (fitness counts matches, p.6); if no bit matches, a convention of inverting the 7th/8th bit is used (p.8).
- Key/location selection: key K=(S,G): seed S plus "guide sequence" G. GA host-pixel sequence. Key statement on the role of the GA: "the only goal of the genetic algorithm in our proposal is to establish a random walk" (p.6) and "any random walk propose by the GA can be used" (p.9). So the GA is a seeded random-position generator by the authors' own description.
- Data: 30 medical images as secrets, hidden in three natural covers (Landscape 512x512, Flower 25x25, Mona Lisa 408x349; Table 2, p.7). "30 medical images were used" (p.8). Not MRI covers; no subjects; no split.
- Security: no detector. "the existing steganalysis techniques cannot be applied to this image" (p.9); PSNR infinity, SSIM 1 (p.8); security argued as key-search complexity O(wh|G|) (p.9). RANDOM baseline under trained detector: not found in text. Matched control: not found in text. Seeds/CIs: not found in text (a "Seed" column in Table 2 is the GA RNG seed, not repeated experiments).
- Position choice vs detectability: asserted irrelevant because nothing is modified: "our method is not vulnerable to statistical attacks" (p.9).
- Adversarial pre-emption argument: the paper treats the GA output as equivalent to a random walk, which agrees in spirit with a GA-equals-random null. It is a design argument, no experiment, no detector, no modification. Pre-empts nothing empirical.

### B2. Damghani, Mofrad, Damghani 2021, file "IET Image Processing - 2021 - Damghani - Medical JPEG image steganography ... .pdf" (10 pp.)
- Title (p.1): "Medical JPEG image steganography method according to the distortion reduction criterion based on an imperialist competitive algorithm". Authors: Hamidreza Damghani, Farshid Babapour Mofrad, Leila Damghani. IET Image Processing 2021;15:705-714. DOI as printed (p.1): 10.1049/ipr2.12055. Received 16 Jan 2020, accepted 8 Oct 2020.
- Embedding: JPEG DCT-domain LSB of nonzero AC coefficients with syndrome coding (H x Y = m); costs from J-UNIWARD (p.4); cost adjustment by inter-block neighbour consistency (CMD-inspired, pp.3-5).
- Selection: Imperialist Competitive Algorithm chooses the LSB pattern per block, fitness uses embedding cost rho (Eq. 21, p.6); "Initialisation of ICA by a random method" (p.6). This is a metaheuristic optimiser of the embedding configuration, not a stego key.
- Data: "we chose three brains magnetic resonance imaging images" for illustration, 512x512 (p.7). Security set: "All the 1000 images are JPEG with three quality factors 75, 85, 95" (p.9); source and whether medical: not found in text. Subject-level split: not found in text.
- Security: JSRM steganalysis, "detection errors Eoob" vs J-UNIWARD at 0.1 to 0.5 bpnzAC (Fig. 9, p.9). Result: "our best performance is in 0.5 embedding payload by about 7.2%" (pp.9-10). Training/testing size, classifier settings, repetitions: not found in text (Eoob implies an out-of-bag ensemble classifier, not stated). Other metric: percent of nonzero coefficients changed (Table 2, p.9). Seeds/CIs: not found in text.
- RANDOM-key baseline under trained detector: not found in text. Matched control: partial only: Table 2 compares the percentage of changed coefficients across methods (e.g. 3.06 vs 3.66 vs 3.11 at 0.2), not matched changes. Comparators are J-UNIWARD and UED, not random.
- Position choice and detectability: "most changes have occurred in the edges and border areas" (p.8); the abstract says smooth areas in medical images "will make the changes more visible if they are manipulated" (p.1), but no smooth-versus-textured experiment is run.
- Adversarial pre-emption argument: an optimiser of embedding choices measured with a feature-based detector (JSRM) shows a detection-error gain over a cost baseline, which is the direction of our feature-detector finding. But the baseline is J-UNIWARD, not a random key; JPEG, not spatial LSB/PVD; no CI; no CNN. Partial context only.

### B3. Karakis 2023 (MI-STEG), file "MI-STEG_ A Medical Image Steganalysis Framework Based on Ensemble Deep Learning.pdf" (18 pp.)
- Title (p.1): "MI-STEG: A Medical Image Steganalysis Framework Based on Ensemble Deep Learning". Author: Rukiye Karakis. Venue: Computers, Materials & Continua, 2023, vol. 74, no. 3, pp. 4649-4666 (page heads). DOI as printed (p.1): 10.32604/cmc.2023.035881. Received 8 Sep 2022, accepted 26 Oct 2022.
- Embedding: steganalysis paper. Stego made with S-UNIWARD, HUGO, MIPOD (binghamton code) at 0.1 to 1.0 bpp; sequential LSB at 1.0 bpp only to pre-train (p.5).
- Selection/key: none studied. "random data in the range of 1.0-0.1 bpp payloads were hidden" (p.4). Not a GA paper.
- Data: brain MRI (T1/T2/FLAIR, axial/coronal/sagittal): 15 epilepsy plus 15 healthy, and 30 focal epilepsy patients (p.6). "The dataset combining 5961 images was divided into training and validation sets ... 90% and 10%" (p.6): an image/slice-level split; subject-level split: not found in text. Test set: "a different dataset containing 3064 T1-weighted brain MR images" (p.6), disjoint by source (cover-source mismatch). Images resized to 224x224 (p.6).
- Security evaluation (as detector): 11 pre-trained DenseNet/ResNet/Inception CNNs with transfer learning down payloads, averaged ensemble; SGD, 20 epochs, "Five-fold cross-validation was used" (p.8). Metrics: accuracy, specificity, precision, recall, F1, R (pp.9-10). Result example: S-UNIWARD 0.2 bpp accuracy 0.979 and 0.1 bpp 0.807 (Tables 4,5, p.14).
- Random baseline, matched control, GA key: not found in text. Seeds/CIs: not found in text.
- Claim to flag: "Developing an ensemble-based DL framework for medical image steganalysis for the first time in literature" (p.4), yet the paper itself cites an earlier SVM and a CNN study (Ozcan [40], Karakis [2], p.13).
- Adversarial pre-emption argument: prior art for CNN steganalysis on brain MRI with adaptive embedders; we must not claim first CNN steganalysis of brain MRI stego. It says nothing about key choice, so it does not pre-empt the GA-versus-random null. Its high CNN accuracy for content-adaptive embedders at 0.2 bpp supports that a CNN is a strong detector on MRI.

### B4. Özcan, Karakış, Güler, SIU 2020, file "Steganalysis on Medical Images with Support Vector Machine.pdf" (4 pp.; Turkish body)
- Title (p.1): "Medikal Görüntüler Üzerinde Destek Vektör Makinesi ile Steganaliz / Steganalysis on Medical Images with Support Vector Machine". Authors (p.1): Fatmanur Betül Maroof Özcan, Rukiye Karakış, İnan Güler. Venue and year: printed only as the IEEE footer "978-1-7281-7206-4/20/$31.00 ©2020 IEEE"; the conference name and DOI are not in the page text. PDF metadata gives "2020 28th Signal Processing and Communications Applications Conference (SIU)" and 10.1109/SIU49456.2020.9302235 (metadata, not printed text).
- Embedding: six LSB variants, 5 payloads (0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1 bpp): LSB1 start top-left row-wise; LSB2 start bottom-right reverse; LSB3 column-wise from top-left; LSB4 from bottom-right upward; LSB5 only pixels below the mean intensity (background); LSB6 only pixels above the mean (brain) (p.2). Message = DICOM header patient data, repeated to reach the payload (p.2).
- Key/location: fixed scan orders and an intensity-region rule; no key, no random order, no optimiser.
- Data: 6778 DICOM brain MR images (p.2); augmentation applied (flips, sharpening, gamma, rotation 10 degrees, translation), then "61086 ... training, 29364 ... test" (Table I, p.2: training 31086 cover + 30000 stego; test 13864 cover + 15500 stego), so augmentation before the split; subject-level split: not found in text. 30 stego images per cover (6 methods x 5 payloads), p.3.
- Security: 216 features from DCT-based neighbour-connection densities (Liu 2011), SVM RBF/linear/polynomial (pp.3-4). Table II (p.4): polynomial kernel accuracy 0.9928, AUC 0.9928, R 0.9856; RBF 0.9886; linear 0.9643. PSNR at 1 bpp for the six methods 66.35, 66.36, 66.37, 66.37, 67.48, 72.74 dB (p.4). Per-method or per-payload detection: not found in text (all pooled).
- Random baseline, matched control, seeds/CIs: not found in text (single run).
- Adversarial pre-emption argument: it is the only paper in the set that changes WHERE bits go (scan order; background versus brain region) in brain MRI under a trained detector. But results are pooled across the six placements, so no position effect can be read off; no random-key arm. The background (LSB5) versus brain (LSB6) split resembles a smooth-versus-textured contrast, but it is never reported separately. Not a pre-emption; also a warning that a pooled, augmented, image-level split inflates accuracy.

### B6. Mansour and Abdelrahim, file "Mansour.pdf" (20 pp.)

Venue, year, volume, DOI: not found in text (PDF metadata only: Word 2007 creation date April 2018, author field "shyamani").

### B9. Aljughaiman and Alrawashdeh, PSO paper, file "Optimization-driven steganographic system based on fused maps and blowfish encryption.pdf" (29 pp.)
- Title (p.1): "Optimization-driven steganographic system based on fused maps and blowfish encryption". Authors: Ahmed Aljughaiman, Rana Alrawashdeh. Scientific Reports (2026) 16:4922. DOI as printed (p.1): 10.1038/s41598-026-35556-9. Received 28 Jul 2025, accepted 7 Jan 2026.
- Embedding: Blowfish-encrypted bitstream, 1 bit per selected pixel by "priority-guided Least Significant Bits (LSB) substitution" (pp.1, 7).
- Selection: fused map = alpha*entropy + (1-alpha)*|Laplacian| (Eq. 3, p.7), exponent gamma, pixels ranked by descending priority (p.7). PSO (N=18, 25 iterations, w=0.72, c1=c2=1.49; Table 1, p.8) "to find the best values for parameters alpha and gamma" (p.8) with fitness = MSE(I,S) (Eq. 4, p.8). Note a discrepancy: the abstract and objectives say PSO optimises "embedding location selection and ordering" (pp.1-2) and index "permutations" (p.8), but the specified search variables are only alpha and gamma. No key-derived random order; "keyed permutation" appears only as a future mitigation (p.23).
- Data: BOSSbase (cover, 30 images for quality tables, N=30 on p.12) and USC-SIPI (secret) (p.5). Not medical, no subjects.
- Security: Xu-Net and Ye-Net trained from scratch on "160 training pairs and 20 validation and test pairs" (pp.14,16); at 0.1/0.2/0.4/1.0 bpp. Xu-Net AUC with PSO 0.5025, 0.5025, 0.4900, 0.5425 and without PSO 0.5075, 0.5000, 0.4900, 0.5700 (Tables 7, 8, pp.18, 20); Ye-Net AUC 0.4925 to 0.5100 with and without PSO, accuracy 50% with every stego classified as cover (pp.16-17, 22, 24). RS mean 0.6651/0.6633/0.6656/0.5921 with PSO (Table 11, p.25) vs 0.6651/0.6634/0.6559/0.5906 without (Table 12, p.26). Authors concede: "The selection channel operates deterministically, which makes it vulnerable to RS analysis, while our PSO objective focuses on minimizing MSE instead of RS evidence" (p.23).
- RANDOM-key baseline under trained detector: not found in text. Matched control: not found in text. Seeds/CIs: not found in text (one deterministic split; "mean±std" in imperceptibility tables over N=30 images only).
- Adversarial pre-emption argument (strongest in this paper): a PSO-versus-no-PSO comparison under trained Xu-Net, Ye-Net and RS shows no detectability change from the optimiser (both rows equal within noise). That is consistent in direction with a "metaheuristic does not change detectability" null. It does not pre-empt ours because the comparator is a deterministic priority map (not a random key), the detectors are at chance for both arms so the test has no power (floor effect), 20 test pairs, natural images, no CIs, no operator matching, no MRI.

### B11. Alrawashdeh, Rahman, Aljughaiman, Niazi, file "Texture attention-guided adaptive LSB steganography with U-Net model and optional GA tuning integrated with ablation study.pdf" (55 pp.)
- Title (p.1): "Texture attention-guided adaptive LSB steganography with U-Net model and optional GA tuning integrated with ablation study". Authors: Rana Alrawashdeh, Mahfuzur Rahman, Ahmed Aljughaiman, Mahmood Niazi. Scientific Reports, "Article in Press" (volume and pages not printed); received 3 Nov 2025, accepted 27 Aug 2026. DOI as printed (p.1): 10.1038/s41598-026-69569-1.
- Embedding: adaptive LSB depth 1 to 4 bits per pixel from an attention map: thresholds 0.3/0.6/0.8, "low-attention areas (below 0.3) receive only 1 bit" and "high-attention regions (above 0.8) receive up to 4 bits" (p.13, Eq. 5 p.14). Attention map = ResNet50 texture energy fused with a distance transform (Eq. 4, p.11); a small U-Net (419,873 parameters) predicts it (p.12).
- GA role: tunes three thresholds and bit levels; fitness 0.40 capacity + 0.35 PSNR + 0.25 SSIM (Table 4, p.15; p.16). GA population 100, 150 generations, "random_seed 2025" (Table 4). No detectability term and no key-derived location order (no stego key described).

- Security: Xu-Net, Ye-Net, SR-Net, matched cover-stego pairs "up to 400", 70/30 random split with seed 42, 30 epochs, 256x256 center crop (p.40). So their GA calibration reproduces the plain baseline operating point.
- Seeds/CIs: "the current evaluation relies on a single random seed and does not quantify variability across multiple independent training and optimization runs" (p.51). Detection has no CI.
- Statement on position choice: "When the objective is to achieve high resistance against to steganalysis, embedding is typically performed in edge or textured regions" (p.2), asserted from literature; not tested.
- Adversarial pre-emption argument: GA plus ablation plus three CNN detectors plus an explicit admission that GA gives no PSNR gain over a simple baseline (p.50). Still no random key, no matched control, no feature-based detector, no medical data, security for one arm only at chance. Does not pre-empt.

## 3. Net verdict for the narrow claim

NOT pre-empted by any of the 11 papers. Nothing in this group has all of: a GA/metaheuristic pixel key, a random-key arm, operator-matched or modification-count-matched controls, trained feature-based and CNN detectors applied to every arm, brain MRI, subject-disjoint splits, multiple seeds, and cluster-bootstrap CIs.

Closest structural precedents, in order:
1. Aljughaiman and Alrawashdeh ACO (B10): has a random-index mode and CNN steganalysers, but the random arm is judged on PSNR only, the detectors are at chance everywhere (floor effect, 20 test pairs), natural images, one split.
2. Aljughaiman and Alrawashdeh PSO (B9): optimiser versus no-optimiser under Xu-Net/Ye-Net/RS, all indistinguishable from chance. Supports the "optimiser does not move detectability" direction only weakly, because the detectors never learn the task.
3. Damghani (B2): a metaheuristic optimisation gives a +7.2% Eoob gain over J-UNIWARD under JSRM on JPEG; the only feature-based-detector gain from an optimiser in the group, but versus a cost baseline, not random, and JPEG.
4. Karakis MI-STEG (B3) and Özcan SVM (B4): prior art that brain-MRI stego can be detected by SVM and CNN; B4 is the only one that varies placement (scan order, background versus brain) but pools results.

Where our wording must be careful:
- Do not claim to be the first brain-MRI steganalysis study with a CNN (B3) or SVM (B4).
- Do not claim that "random index versus metaheuristic index" was never compared at all; B10 compares them on PSNR. Say "never compared under trained detectors with matched controls".
- Do not claim that GA-versus-baseline has never been reported as having no benefit; B11 reports that the GA calibration reproduces the Fixed LSB PSNR exactly (GA Only = Fixed LSB, p.46) and that learned attention shows no PSNR gain over an edge baseline (p.50). That is a PSNR-domain null, not a detectability null.
- The "position or texture choice changes detectability" belief is stated as literature background by B11 (p.2), B2 (p.8), and B5 (p.37), but none tests it with a trained detector against a random arm, and none shows detector dependence (feature-based versus CNN). The detector-dependence finding (feature-based sees a difference, a full-budget CNN does not) is not found in any of the 11 papers; the only comparison across detector families is B3 vs B4 across different data and different embedders, which is not a controlled test.

## 4. Disagreements and additions versus rounds 5 and 6

I compared after finishing my own notes. Agreement is high on all verdicts (no paper pre-empts). Differences and additions:

1. Round 5 (B10, ACO): agree on PSNR-only random arm and chance-level detectors. Addition: row 5 (saliency_topk) failed extraction while random and ACO succeeded (Table 2, p.11), and the paper concedes "no significant" image-quality effect of index mode (p.11). Round 5 gives the PSNR gap "less than 0.5 dB" (agree).
2. Round 5 (B9, PSO): agree no random arm. Addition: the paper does contain a PSO-versus-no-PSO detector comparison (Tables 7 to 12), both at chance, and RS values nearly identical; round 5 lists the RS and AUC numbers but not this with/without contrast. Also the abstract says PSO optimises "location selection and ordering" while the method optimises only alpha and gamma (pp.1, 8); round 5 notes alpha, gamma but not the mismatch.

Additions: (a) every condition realises BPP 0.125, so the "0.40 BPP" in the security table is a nominal target; (b) GA Only equals Fixed LSB to the third decimal in PSNR (Table 29, p.46), meaning the GA collapses to the baseline operating point; (c) the two split descriptions (160/40/200 vs 307/76/386) are inconsistent in the paper. I did not independently confirm round 6's quote "All methods were evaluated using the same realized payload" (p.22); my pass did not retain it verbatim, so treat that quote as unverified by me.
